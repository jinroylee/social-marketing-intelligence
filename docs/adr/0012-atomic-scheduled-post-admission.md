# Commit scheduled new-post admission with canonical evidence

Status: accepted

Scheduled queries overlap and workers may execute concurrently. Counting fetched records would charge malformed or denied evidence and duplicate identities; counting publication membership after canonical writes would permit excess new posts into shared evidence. Durable reservations made before mapping would require a separate recovery protocol after every failure.

Admit unique eligible new posts at canonical application, after normalization/current process-policy checks and deterministic canonical identity locks, before creating entities or derivations. The schedule owner receives only normalized identities and the canonical owner's locked known-identity result. Its workspace/day row serializes remaining capacity across disjoint post identities. The admission disposition, canonical writes and source applied/skipped status commit on the existing task ownership connection. Rollback removes both debit and effects; no separate reservation survives a crash. A committed debit survives a lost acknowledgment and reopens through the same observation identity. Ingestion owns canonical SQL; schedule admission owns its own ledger SQL. Neither owner reads the other's tables to approximate authority or novelty.

A post has one platform/namespace/native/synthetic identity. Exactly one stable content identity is required per mapped record. A previously unknown identity consumes one successful new admission. Known-record refreshes and identities already admitted/refreshed that day retain distinct dispositions without additional debit, allowing legitimate new observations. Unknown identities beyond the cap and records lacking a stable post identity retain evidence/checkpoints with skipped canonical status, making fewer-record outcomes visible. Invalid normalization or denied processing retains existing failure/quarantine semantics and never reaches admission. This counts successful canonical admission, independently of later publication availability or policy withdrawal; deleting a keyword or withdrawing support cannot silently refund a day’s load.

The immutable occurrence fixes its accounting day/timezone, including delayed execution after midnight. Current-day configuration edits may raise or lower the effective cap; used identities survive every edit, restart and removal/re-registration. Lowering below used retains history with remaining zero. Older days retain their last applicable configuration. Configuration and missing-day bootstrap share the schedule lock; bootstrap chooses the latest configuration recorded before that day's fixed end, and cap versions advance monotonically. Old queued contexts cannot overwrite a newer cap. Upgrade initializes day metadata without inventing retrospective debits for effects committed before enforcement. Historical task scope remains immutable; current enforcement and usage are read separately.

This adds short day-row contention at canonical commits while keeping source I/O outside transactions. Early collection may still spend independent request/record/cost allowance for an excluded result, and the system promises no daily fill or per-keyword yield. Post identity is source identity, not semantic similarity; alternate namespaces remain distinct. External provider execution, live scheduling and monetary control retain separate owners and limits.
