# Social Marketing Intelligence

Shared language for evidence-backed market investigation and creator campaign decisions.

## Language

**Brand**:
The commercial identity under which products are marketed. A brand is distinct from the company that owns or operates it.

**Product**:
A marketed item or product family associated with a brand. A reference to the family does not establish an exact version, listing, variant or shade.

**Caption interpretation**:
A derived claim about a reference or meaning in observed caption text, with supporting evidence and review status. A literal mention does not by itself establish the author's stance or verify the claim being discussed.

**Target market**:
The geographic market a campaign intends to address. It is distinct from a product's origin, a post's language, and the measured location of a creator's audience.

**Market monitoring**:
Ongoing observation and investigation of accessible market evidence that informs marketers and the shared knowledge. It operates independently of campaign initiation and creator selection.

**Market signal**:
An evidence-backed observation or interpretation of a pattern or change in accessible market activity, with its time window and coverage limits. A signal can inform a marketer's understanding without initiating a campaign.

**Monitoring investigation**:
A saved topic or product question with an evidence summary and explicit sample limits. An investigation can describe a fixed snapshot without establishing market growth or change over time.

**Market insight**:
A versioned finding or summary answering a monitoring investigation, with its supporting evidence, relevant times, and coverage limits. It may inform a campaign brief without initiating a campaign.

**Campaign**:
An internal planning context created by a marketer, described by a campaign brief and associated decision records. Its creation does not mean that an external campaign has been activated.

**Campaign brief**:
A marketer-defined statement of the brand/product scope, objective, target market, platforms, and at least one creator-selection criterion. It is the starting point for campaign planning and exploratory creator comparison even when commercial details remain unknown.

**Executable criteria interpretation**:
A saved, versioned reading of campaign needs pinned to an exact brief and original input. Model-generated additions retain their automated basis and preserve the human constraints; they are distinct from verified source facts and grant no task authority. A later assessment explicitly selects that immutable interpretation.

**Creator**:
A person, group, or organization that creates content, distinct from any particular platform account. Accounts are linked to a creator through corroborated evidence or a recorded marketer correction.

**Creator shortlist**:
A saved campaign-specific collection of recommended or curated platform accounts, optionally associated with corroborated creator identities, with supporting reasons and evidence. It may be saved automatically without human review or approval; inclusion does not mean the creator has agreed to participate or has been contracted.

**Ranked recommendation**:
An ordered set of candidate accounts assessed against a campaign's criteria, with its ranking basis, supporting evidence and explicit gaps. The installed bounded model method orders deterministically eligible current-KB accounts using constrained supported-criterion judgments; its generated relative fit is distinct from source verification or human endorsement. It can be saved automatically as a shortlist; optional human curation remains a separate judgment.

**Campaign coverage assessment**:
A recorded judgment of whether the supported candidate set meets an explicit quantity, relevance and freshness policy for a campaign. Its reasons and limits describe the assessed evidence; sufficiency does not establish complete market coverage or verified campaign outcomes.

**Creator discovery**:
Finding candidate creators who are not already in the marketer's working set for a campaign. Discovery produces candidates for evaluation; it does not establish their suitability or availability.

**Keyword discovery**:
Finding source records through an explicit query, subject scope and time window with stated selection and coverage limits. A supplied reference or a successful profile lookup does not establish query discovery or complete market coverage.

**Source retrieval receipt**:
A record of the source operation and evidence actually returned, with its coverage and unknowns. It is distinct from a final billing report, source-use permission and a usable knowledge publication.

**Discovery avoidance decision**:
A recorded choice to omit unnecessary retrieval or processing based on the exact prior query, source context, capture freshness and usable evidence. Avoidance creates no new source observation or knowledge publication and grants no broader acquisition authority.

**Supplementation decision**:
A recorded choice to retrieve one explicitly authorized missing field or stop further acquisition, based on existing support, freshness, permission, capability, expected information value and resource cost. Additional source support retains its own provenance and does not establish equivalence between source identities.

**Registered scheduled keyword**:
An operator-authorized recurring discovery query with an explicit subject scope and a stable identity. It is independent of monitoring topics and does not restrict separately approved on-demand investigation.

**Scheduled occurrence**:
One keyword's due discovery within a particular accounting day, retaining the configuration and initiating authority that applied to it. Later keyword or configuration changes do not replace its history.

**Scheduled new-post allowance**:
The shared daily admission limit for unique eligible new posts found across registered scheduled keywords. A new post is a previously unknown eligible post identity; a later observation of known content is a refresh. Repeated identities consume no additional admission. The allowance is distinct from requests, measured cost, per-keyword yield and guaranteed throughput.

**Creator comparison**:
Evaluating candidate creators against the same campaign needs using their available evidence and explicit unknowns. The candidates may come from discovery or the marketer's existing working set.

**Comparison draft**:
A provisional comparison of candidate accounts against an exact campaign brief and identified evidence, with supported judgments, reasons and unknowns. Its interpretations do not become human-reviewed facts merely because the result is saved.

**Shortlist revision**:
A saved version of a campaign's recommended or curated candidate accounts, their ordering where applicable, and the decision basis. Its automated or human authorship and any optional human review remain explicit; later changes create a successor revision.

**Recommendation regeneration**:
An explicit marketer request to assess current permitted knowledge against the current campaign needs and save a successor shortlist revision. It preserves earlier recommendations and does not promise a better result or imply human endorsement.

**Enrichment proposal**:
An assistant's proposed acquisition of missing, stale or incomplete knowledge, with a stated purpose, refresh/fill/expansion intent and subject/search scope. An immutable saved revision identifies the exact scope and resource limits a human can approve. It requires attributable human approval before becoming a submitted enrichment task.

**Enrichment approval**:
An attributable human action authorizing one exact saved proposal revision and scope in its workspace. It is distinct from generated text, proposal creation and task submission; a single approval authorizes at most one logical task.

**Enrichment task**:
A scoped acquisition of additional evidence for the shared knowledge, initiated by a configured schedule, campaign coverage need or approved assistant proposal. Its completion distinguishes retrieved material from knowledge actually made available for use.

**Task execution authority**:
Temporary ownership of one admitted task's local effects by a worker. Handoff retains the task's original approval/scope, attempt, ingestion run and committed publication; owning execution grants no broader acquisition authority.

**Task completion event**:
A durable identity for one enrichment task’s terminal outcome, committed with that outcome. The local inbox presents historical coverage separately from current permitted publication support; delivery retries retain the same event.

**Knowledge answer**:
A response to a marketer’s question with identified knowledge support, relevant dates, uncertainty and missing information. Relevance selection and a source claim do not establish independent verification or complete coverage.

**Knowledge publication**:
A versioned availability of ingested evidence and supported interpretations for knowledge-base queries. It identifies the source support and transformations that make an update usable.

**Monitoring observation**:
A recent source observation used for timely monitoring and anomaly detection. It can support an alert without every observed post becoming a fully modeled knowledge-base entity.

**Anomaly alert**:
A retained notification of a significant quantitative or semantic anomaly, with its evidence, comparison context, method and uncertainty. It does not itself authorize further external research or campaign creation.

**Measurement plan**:
A descriptive proposal for measuring a campaign's objective, with intended outcome definitions, sources, periods, matching and attribution assumptions, and known gaps. It is distinct from observed results and does not establish causal lift.

**Decision note**:
Attributable human feedback, assumptions or learning attached to a campaign and relevant shortlist revision. Its author-supplied statements remain distinct from independently observed evidence.

**Platform account**:
An identity on a particular source platform under which content and account metrics are observed. It is distinct from the creator associated with it and from its mutable display name or handle.

**Source reference**:
A supplied or discovered pointer to possible evidence, such as a content URL or account handle. A reference does not establish that the resource was retrieved, is available, or belongs to a verified identity.

**Source observation**:
Evidence obtained through a particular acquisition route, with its source, acquisition context, relevant times, coverage, and permitted uses. An observation is distinct from the real-world account or content it describes; several observations may describe the same entity.

**Assertion**:
An observed, derived, or human-supplied claim about an entity, retaining its evidence and relevant context. Conflicting assertions can coexist; a preferred current value does not erase the conflict or make missing evidence certain.

**Metric observation**:
A measurement with its metric definition, value, subject, relevant time or window, and source evidence. The same label from different sources does not by itself establish comparable measurements.

**Cost scope**:
An operator-configured service/provider, currency and accounting period with explicit metered or forecast-only classification. Synthetic unit/rate measurements establish simulated resource costs, not commercial charges; unknown coverage, forecasts and outstanding commitments remain separate from measured actuals and enforced admission.

**Cost operation**:
A stable workspace/request-bound accounting identity with actor, method and cost scope, optionally validated against an exact acquisition task/attempt. Equivalent model operations retain attribution without requiring an acquisition task.


**Synthetic monetary admission**:
A shared allowance decision for simulated metered work within one explicit cost scope. Its monetary ceiling includes measured spending and all outstanding commitments; task approval remains separate, and forecast-only scopes receive no monetary enforcement claim.

**Cost reconciliation**:
An attributable final measurement that identifies the outstanding cost portions it settles while retaining their original evidence. A reconciled cost does not establish that a missing service response or usable knowledge has been recovered.

**Meter recovery**:
An attributable confirmation that an expected cost meter is healthy again, distinct from the final accounting for its failed measurements. Restored meter health does not make an unresolved charge free.
